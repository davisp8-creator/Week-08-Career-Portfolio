Does positional encoding matter more than seed noise?

Patrick Davis · Single-GPU practitioner portfolio · Option A (refined from a Week 7 ablation) · Repo: github.com/davisp8-creator/Week-08-Career-Portfolio

Question and budget

For a 69k-parameter Transformer forecasting the next 24 h of rural-feeder demand, does the positional encoding (none, sinusoidal, learned, RoPE) change test error by more than run-to-run noise? One free Colab T4, 4 arms × 5 seeds = 20 runs, about 8 minutes of wall-clock. Synthetic data; fp32; one factor changes.

  • Position information matters: removing it costs 12.6 kW (about 22 seed SDs) and loses to a repeat-yesterday baseline (12.37 kW). Holm-adjusted p = 7e-6.
  • The other three tie, but a tie is not equality. Five seeds could only detect a true gap of about 0.26–0.32 kW (3–4%) at 80% power. Settling a 0.1 kW gap would take about 36 seeds per arm, roughly an hour on a T4.
  • RoPE costs more: +14% latency, +18% training time, +42 MB on the T4 (+16% and +20% in the first session); +34% latency on CPU. Retrained 3 more times: per-run MAE bit-identical, latency spread at most 0.0004 ms.
Hardware: Colab Tesla T4 14.6 GB (sm_75), driver 580.82.07, CUDA 13.0 build, torch 2.11.0, Python 3.13.16; 2-vCPU host. Seq 168→24, batch 128 / 256 / 512 (train / latency / eval), 10 epochs.

Limitations to discount by

  • Synthetic data and one data seed: error bars exclude data variance; 8.4 kW is not field accuracy.
  • CPU and T4 runs differ in Python, torch, numpy and pandas as well as hardware. T4 repeats ran back to back on one VM; latency moved 2.4% between two sessions and no other GPU was tried.
  • One tiny model, probably launch-bound and unprofiled; the cost ranking may flip at scale. Hyperparameters were not tuned per arm.
